Measure

Put the Gateway at the seat with the microphone at ear height, and keep the room quiet. The speaker plays a 2.5 s sweep (one per driver when it is bi-amped, the other output silent), from −30 dBFS up until it is clear of the room noise. With Speaker EQ off (the default) it measures the speaker as it is in the room, for the correction: its filters, level, polarity and delay are left out, only a bi-amp crossover and the subwoofer's low-pass stay (they protect the drivers). Switched on, it measures it as it plays, to compare with the correction. Measure every speaker from two or three positions 40–60 cm apart. A subwoofer is always measured mono. Nothing changes on the speakers.

Response

The levels are relative to the level played, so speakers, drivers and positions compare directly. The tick boxes choose what the graph shows, and the correction below uses the ones shown. The graph shows what reaches the seat in the 500 ms after the direct sound: the speaker with the room. Measurements with the speaker's EQ on are drawn dashed. The INMP441 is not calibrated and is specified from 60 Hz to 15 kHz, so with it the curves end at 16 kHz. The measurements are kept in this browser only.

Correction

From the measurements of that speaker shown above with its EQ off (several positions are averaged). Below about 500 Hz it works on what you hear in the room and cuts its peaks precisely; dips are left, or raised a little at most, because a dip from a reflection does not fill in with more power. Above about 500 Hz it works on the speaker's own sound (the frequency-dependent window) with broad filters only, since the room's reflections there change with every move of your head. It never corrects below where the speaker itself rolls off, nor above the microphone's range. A subwoofer is corrected up to 300 Hz at most, through the Gateway's crossover. With bi-amp each driver gets its own filters, and the tweeter's level is matched to the woofer's. Apply sends the result to the speaker now, Apply & Save also keeps it after a restart. On the speaker it replaces the EQ bands and levels of its outputs and keeps their high- and low-pass bands; its outputs, crossover, polarity and delay stay as they are. To check: measure again with Speaker EQ switched on; the graph shows it next to the expected result. Afterwards run Calibrate on the Speakers page, as the correction changes the speaker's level.

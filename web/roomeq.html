Measure each speaker from two or three positions 40–60 cm apart around your seat; Room EQ averages them. A bi-amp speaker is measured driver by driver, both drivers together, or all three in a row.

Measurement settings

Put the Gateway at the seat with the microphone at ear height, and keep the room quiet. As in REW: the speaker plays an exponential sweep (one per driver when it is bi-amped, the other output silent) at the Level you set, its RMS (the peak is 3 dB higher); Length and Repetitions (averaged) each give almost 3 dB less noise per doubling, a longer sweep better than more of them. It sweeps from half the start to twice the end frequency. Check levels first plays 3 s of pink noise at that level: as REW asks, the microphone should be between −30 and −12 dBFS RMS, with at least 6 dB headroom to full scale. The speaker's limit is kept on the Gateway: neither the sweep nor the noise is ever louder. Export for REW saves the last measurement's impulse responses as WAV (REW: File → Import → Import impulse response). It measures the speaker as it is in the room, for the correction: its filters, level, polarity and delay are left out, only a bi-amp crossover and the subwoofer's low-pass stay (they protect the drivers). After Apply & Save the page offers a check: the same speaker measured as it now plays, with its new EQ, drawn over the one before. Measure every speaker from two or three positions 40–60 cm apart. A subwoofer is always measured mono. Nothing changes on the speakers.

Response
Move over the graph for the values at a frequency; drag across it to zoom in, double-click (or tap twice) for the whole range.

The levels are relative to the level played, so speakers, drivers and positions compare directly. The tick boxes choose what the graph shows, and the correction below uses the ones shown. The graph shows what reaches the seat in the 500 ms after the direct sound: the speaker with the room. Measurements with the speaker's EQ on are drawn dashed. The INMP441 is not calibrated and is specified from 60 Hz to 15 kHz, so with it the curves end at 16 kHz. The measurements are kept in this browser only.

Correction
toHz

From the measurements of that speaker shown above with its EQ off (several positions are averaged). Below about 500 Hz it works on what you hear in the room and cuts its peaks precisely. As in REW, everything above the target line is cut down to it; below it, a dip no deeper than Individual max boost is raised towards the line, and a deeper one is left as it is, because a deep dip from a reflection does not fill in with more power. No single filter raises more than Individual max boost (0 dB: cuts only), and all filters together raise no frequency by more than Overall max boost: with a high individual limit (say +12 dB) and a low overall one (say +3 dB) deeper dips are raised too, but only partly. A narrow dip is never raised. How narrow a filter may be is set as in REW: Allow narrow filters below 200 Hz allows Q up to 10 there, for a room's resonances (with averaged positions); above 200 Hz Q is 5 at most, or, with Vary max Q above 200 Hz, from 10 at 200 Hz down to 3 at 10 kHz and above. Without narrow filters, Q is 5 at most everywhere. A boost is never narrower than Q 3. Allow low shelf and Allow high shelf (off by default) add a shelf at that end of the range, as REW does, only where the curve stays above or below the line for at least half an octave there; the number beside each is the largest shelf gain either way (a boost still within the max boost limits). Match range is where filters may go at all (REW's match range); inside it, the curve's own crossings of the line still decide where correction starts and ends. It may use all 24 bands of an output (but for its own high- and low-pass bands, which it keeps; a bi-amp crossover is apart from them). Above the filters it says how much quieter (or louder) the speaker plays than before the correction, measured as Calibrate measures level. Flatness target is how close it goes: it stops once nothing is further than that from the line, and drops a filter of less than half of it; a wider one (up to ±6 dB) gives fewer, broader filters that depend less on where the microphone was. As REW, it works on the room's response (the 500 ms window) at all frequencies, smoothed with REW's Var smoothing; Direct sound above 500 Hz uses the speaker's own sound there instead (the frequency-dependent window). The target type is REW's: Full range (an LF cutoff and slope, 0 for flat), Bass limited (the bass management high-pass, 80 Hz) or Subwoofer, with REW's room curve (HF fall, LF rise) on top. Drag the dashed target line up or down on the graph to set its level. A ticked Lock keeps that filter as it is when you calculate again; the others fit around it. It corrects only from where the curve first reaches the target line up to where it last does (as REW does), so a speaker's own roll-off is never boosted, and never above the microphone's range. Target level is where the target line sits at 1 kHz, on the graph's scale (dB re the level played): by default the speaker's own level between 200 Hz and 2 kHz (its woofer's with bi-amp), smoothed over one octave as REW does; type another one to move the line, as in REW, and everything above it is cut, below it raised within the max boost limits. It is kept for each speaker in this browser; Auto goes back to the default. The graph shows the target and the expected curve on the measurement's scale; the level of each output below includes the cut that keeps headroom for the boosts. A subwoofer is corrected up to 300 Hz at most, through the Gateway's crossover. With bi-amp each driver gets its own filters, and the tweeter's level is matched to the woofer's, raised by at most Overall max boost (filters and level together). Apply sends the result to the speaker now, Apply & Save also keeps it after a restart. On the speaker it replaces the EQ bands and levels of its outputs and keeps their high- and low-pass bands; its outputs, crossover, polarity and delay stay as they are. After Apply & Save it asks to check: the speaker is measured again with its new EQ (keep the microphone where it was), and the graph draws that curve over the one before, without calculating anything again; a bi-amp speaker is measured driver by driver and then with both together, drawn green against the target they should add up to, which shows a dip or a peak at the crossover; Calculate and the apply buttons rest until a setting or a measurement changes. Afterwards run Calibrate on the Speakers page, as the correction changes the speaker's level.

Driver alignment

For a bi-amp speaker: the delay and polarity of its outputs that make woofer and tweeter add up in phase around the crossover. It measures each driver as the speaker plays it now (its EQ, crossover, polarity and delay on), both on one time base, then tries every delay of the earlier driver from 0 to 2 ms (0.01 ms steps) and both polarities of the tweeter, and keeps the one whose sum is flattest from half to twice the crossover frequency, in the direct sound (a window of 8 periods of the crossover frequency). The proposal is a change from the speaker's current delay and polarity. Nothing changes until you apply it. Measure both together then draws the measured sum over the expected one. The check after Apply & Save above measures the drivers the same way, so it gives a proposal too.
